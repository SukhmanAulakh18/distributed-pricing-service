XVA desk

Should we say yes to this trade, and what does the client's credit cost us?

A corporate client asks the bank for a 5-year equity hedge. Before the trader says yes, the XVA desk must answer three questions in seconds. How much of the client's credit line does it use (PFE)? What will the client's possible default cost us (CVA)? Does a collateral agreement change the answer?

PFE is the bad-case exposure (95th percentile) at each future date. CVA is the price tag on the client defaulting while they owe us money. A CSA is the collateral agreement: when the trade moves in our favour, they hand over cash.

Measured by bench/bench_xva.py on the author's laptop (), with a zero-threshold CSA and a 10-day margin period unless stated. Percentages are computed on this page from the raw benchmark file, on the same scenarios.

Who uses it

Five people ask this system different questions. Each one acts on a different number.

Trader

Decision: can I do this trade, and what do I charge on top of the market price?

Driven by: incremental CVA of the new trade

XVA desk

Decision: how much CVA and DVA P&L do we carry, and what do we hedge?

Driven by: CVA, DVA, bilateral CVA

Credit risk

Decision: does the worst plausible exposure fit inside this client's credit limit?

Driven by: peak PFE at 95%

Collateral and legal

Decision: which CSA terms are worth negotiating: threshold, minimum transfer, margin period?

Driven by: CVA and PFE with and without the CSA

SRE and platform

Decision: will the numbers arrive on time, and the same, even when nodes die?

Driven by: latency, and identical results on any worker count

    Try this

    Margin period zero, thresholds zero: collateral removes almost all exposure.
    Now 20 days. Exposure grows back because the price can move a lot before we can close out, which is why regulators floor it at 10 days for most netting sets.
    Raise both thresholds. The CSA asks for collateral less often and CVA climbs back toward the uncollateralized number.
    Back to a zero-threshold CSA, 10-day margin period, 150 bps.

          Limitations, stated plainly

          • Flat interest rate and flat credit curves; constant volatility; Black-Scholes revaluation.
          • PFE is risk-neutral (drift at the risk-free rate), not a real-world projection.
          • No wrong-way risk: the client's default chance does not depend on the market.
          • Defaults of the bank and the client are independent.
          • Synthetic data. The in-browser model is a teaching sketch with 2,000 scenarios, so its numbers carry sampling noise and are not comparable to the engine's.
